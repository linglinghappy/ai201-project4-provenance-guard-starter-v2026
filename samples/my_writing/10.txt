The reading mentions that the fine lines of a dinosaur fossil skeleton called Sinosauropteryx are not evidence that show Sinosauropteryx have feathers. However, the lecturer states that the reasons made in the reading are unconvincing.

First, the reading article implies that lines in the fossil were not functional structures of a dinosaur. It may be the skin decomposition that was preserved as lines. Nevertheless, the speaker contends that they cannot see any decomposition of fossils buried at the sites. In addition, other animal structures were beautifully well-preserved. As a result, the lines were not the fibers caused by the decomposition.

Second, the reading passage indicates that scientists could not tell from the lines which part of the dinosaur's structure was. Also, it claims that the skeleton was likely to be remains of frills instead of the remains of feathers. Nonetheless, the lecturer argues against the hypothesis by pointing out that there are chemical differences between feathers and frills. Feathers are protein that contains beta-kersci, while frills do not contain specific chemicals. Because the structures of the fossils included the chemicals, the lines are proved to be feathers.

Third, the author of the reading illustrates that the functions of feathers help animals fly or keep their heat. The lines of the fossil were located along the backbones and the tail of the animals, which may be useless for any function. Yet the professor contends that feathers can have other functions. She provides the Peacock as an example. They have colorful feathers in order to attract their mates. Additionally, the analysis showed that the structures are orange and white. Thus, the function of colorful structures was to display.
